# What we know about the game

Facts checked in the game while building the probe, and where each one came from. Last updated 2026-10-16. Game facts about the save format, the clock and finances live in the [tf3-save-format notes](https://github.com/WuphonsReach/tf3-save-format/tree/00c4039160d26f4fd15df0fdaf9466e67c737f7d/docs); links to them here are pinned to one commit (see [README](README.md)).

Test saves: a small one (vegetables and fish, horse carts and ships, road and water only) and a large late-game one (road, rail, tram, water and air, 11 cargo types). Both were played at calendar speed 4x or 0.

## How pay works

- **Spoiled cargo pays full price in the base game.** Fresh, spoiled and mixed carts are all paid the same: the income function's result times a constant of about 0.96426 that the engine applies afterwards (cause unknown).
- **Pay is straight-line distance**: price factor x 3D distance between pickup and delivery (plus a climb term). The unloading point matters, so a cart that stops slightly further away earns slightly more.
- **The income function cannot see freshness.** `baseConfig.economy.cargoIncomeScript` can be replaced by a mod (only one mod at a time can), but it is called once per cargo unit with only `difficultyFactor`, the price factor and the distance. It is called before the unit's arrival event.
- **A delivered unit is paid once, at the consumer, and the pay is shared back along its chain** by a formula we do not know (the player's observation from play; the arrival event is a final delivery and `pickupPoints` lists the legs). The tf3-save-format notes saw the same thing in one delivery: one booking per carrier at one time, 82% to water ([finances.md](https://github.com/WuphonsReach/tf3-save-format/blob/00c4039160d26f4fd15df0fdaf9466e67c737f7d/docs/finances.md), "One delivery paid both lines"). On the large save, ledgers of first-leg vehicles received between 0.05 and 11 times the pay predicted for their own units, which fits a split along the chain.
- **Predicting the pay of a unit in the arrival handler works on the small save** (within about 1%, from the source and target construction positions). On the large save the prediction of a unit's whole pay was not checkable per ledger because of the split, and ledgers dominated by one cargo type showed steady, different ratios (0.8 to 0.86 for air, tram, one rail and one water cargo; 0.59 for another rail cargo); cause open, see [revisit-later](future/revisit-later.md).
- **About 14% more income reaches the player than the arrivals predict** (ratio 1.11 to 1.16 on the small save, 1.36 on the large one); cause unknown.

## Cargo units and freshness

- **Each cargo unit is an entity** (`SimCargo`) with `startTime` (produced), `pickupTime`, a source entity, `deliveryExtensionDuration` and `pickupPoints`: one entry per vehicle and line the unit travelled on, each with its own carrier. `api.engine.util.cargo.isGoodQuality(unit)` tells whether it is spoiled.
- **A unit spoils exactly `timeToDeliverInSeconds x 1.5` after pickup** on the small save (1,687,500 ms for vegetables and fish). Freshness is `1 - (now - pickupTime) / that`; the vehicle window's percentage is the load's average. The flag and the formula agreed on all 82 units checked there. The delivery times per cargo are in [delivery-times.md](https://github.com/WuphonsReach/tf3-save-format/blob/00c4039160d26f4fd15df0fdaf9466e67c737f7d/docs/delivery-times.md), spoilage in [spoilage.md](https://github.com/WuphonsReach/tf3-save-format/blob/00c4039160d26f4fd15df0fdaf9466e67c737f7d/docs/spoilage.md).
- **The formula disagrees with the game's flag for stored cargo.** On the large save, 124 units of one rail cargo and 38 of a road cargo were flagged good although the formula put them at 1.7 to 7 times past the limit. They probably waited in a warehouse (the player reports that a specialized warehouse stops the spoil timer, see [warehouses.md](https://github.com/WuphonsReach/tf3-save-format/blob/00c4039160d26f4fd15df0fdaf9466e67c737f7d/docs/warehouses.md)) or have an extension; open. The third large-save run showed it again: 372 of 2,078 units arrived with negative freshness by the formula and the game flagged 298 of them good (cargo types 6, 20 and 29 averaged -0.22, -1.93 and -4.71). 1,501 units had an extension above zero.
- **All eleven cargo types seen use the same price function.** Each cargo type's `scriptRef` is `incomeFactors.constantFn` with the same parameters (initial price 4, final price 3.2, decay rate 0.01, decay mod 0.1); only the decay start differs (100 for one cargo, 50 for the others). So the steady per-cargo differences in pay on the large save do not come from different price functions.
- **The limits differ by cargo.** The large save showed limits from 1,687,500 ms (fish) to 6,750,000 ms; the probe reads each cargo type's own delivery time.

## What a mod can and cannot do

- **The cargo type's price function cannot be redirected from a mod at run time.** The `scriptRef` is readable through `api.res.cargoTypeRep.get(id)` but changing it had no effect.
- **Game script rules learned the hard way:** `sendCommand` must not be called from `handleEvent` (assertion `!m_betweenChanges`), journal entries must have `time = -1`, `sendCommand` must be called without a result callback from `update` (a callback raises "Callbacks are currently disallowed"), and module-level variables do not survive between calls, so state goes in the game script `state`. State written by the handler is visible to `update`.
- **Asking the engine about a vanished entity aborts the whole event.** A vehicle sold while its cargo was still on the way made `getComponent` raise "Invalid entity" (8 times in 15 minutes on the large save), which lost every unit of that event. Guard lookups with `api.engine.entityExists`, and read the carrier from the pickup point (`PickupPoint.carrier`), not from the vehicle.
- **A journal booking reaches the account.** `api.cmd.makeJournalBookAssetCmd` with an INCOME entry (negative amount) sent from `update` changes the player's balance by exactly the booked amount. Because there is no callback, success is judged from the balance; the entry's carrier is the row it appears under in the Finances tab.
- **Vehicles and lines have their own ledgers.** The first argument of `makeJournalBookAssetCmd` is the entity whose account the entry goes to; a vehicle, a line and the player each carry an `ACCOUNT` component, and an entry booked to one changes only that one. Booking to a vehicle or line works, but the probe no longer does it: pay is shared along the chain by an unknown formula, so the first leg is the wrong place (see [discarded](future/discarded.md)).
- **Time in the journal is the game clock**, not the calendar: the game books on clock-tick periods whatever the calendar speed. A running-cost batch comes every 60,000 clock units and a year is 1,461,000 ([running-costs.md](https://github.com/WuphonsReach/tf3-save-format/blob/00c4039160d26f4fd15df0fdaf9466e67c737f7d/docs/running-costs.md), [calendar.md](https://github.com/WuphonsReach/tf3-save-format/blob/00c4039160d26f4fd15df0fdaf9466e67c737f7d/docs/calendar.md)).

## Test results

- **Deductions land.** On the small save at 4x (8 game-minutes, 104 units, 992,034 in total) every tracked vehicle and line balance moved by pay minus running costs minus the deductions, within 3% of the prediction; the account on screen matched the logged balance apart from the widget lagging a few seconds.
- **Ships, rail and trams are deducted too.** A run with the water limit shrunk to a quarter (a test change, since reverted) deducted 54 ship units (197,529). On the large save, units on road, rail, tram and water were deducted (1,680 units, 18.7 million) with no unit lost between the arrival lines and the bookings. **Air has not been deducted yet:** the cargo plane line delivered at 0.72 to 0.78 freshness, above the probe's full-pay threshold.
- **Chains of three legs work.** Third large-save run (rebuilt probe, 4x, about 4 minutes, 2,078 arrivals): 577 units travelled one leg, 1,242 two and 259 three (water > rail > tram, water > rail > rail, rail > rail > road, road > rail > road); no "Invalid entity" error, 928 units deducted (11.3 million) on road, rail, tram and water, all 928 sent to the player, 535 of 881 entries (61%) visible as an exact step of the balance or income total. Player pay over predicted pay was 1.107 without the deductions (1.145 in the control run with none), so the player-only booking shows no sign of an error. Six screenshots matched the logged balance within 0.003%, two of them exactly. Per-vehicle and per-line ratios are scattered (median 0.41 and 0.61) because those ledgers get only their share of a chain's pay.
- **The panels move in lumps.** The line's balance panel only moves when a whole cart finishes unloading, and the account drops in lumps when running costs are booked for every vehicle together. A screenshot is a rough check; the log is the reference.
