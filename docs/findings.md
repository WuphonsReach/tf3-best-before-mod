# What we know about the game

## In short

- **Key drivers.** The game pays a unit once, in full, whatever its freshness: price factor x distance x the Cargo Income setting x the company's ticket price multiplier. A unit's limit is the game's adjusted delivery time for its cargo, which shrinks as the game years pass (1.5 x the cargo file's time in 1900, 1.4 x in 1916, about 0.75 x by 2191), and freshness is `1 - age / limit`. Cargo that waited in a specialized warehouse has its timer paused, so its age is corrected by `deliveryExtensionDuration`.
- **How we think it works.** The income function cannot see freshness, so the mod lets the engine pay and books a correcting entry (a bonus or a deduction) to the player's account from the arrival event. A booking sent from `update` reaches the account exactly. The mod predicts the unit's pay from the construction positions of its source and target.
- **How sure we are.** Sure: bookings land, bonuses and a full -100% deduction included; chains of three legs work. Fairly sure: the pay prediction (1.001 of the ledger on both flat saves, earned rank 1 and 2) and the freshness formula (it matched the game's flag on every unit checked on the small save). Less sure: the spoil rule for stored cargo; the corrected age matches the flag on every logged unit but is not proven.
- **Unknown.** How the game shares a transferred unit's pay along its chain. Why the player's income exceeds the prediction on some saves. What the game does with an account that stays negative. The exact rule by which the delivery limit shrinks with the years, and whether the spoiled flag flips exactly at the adjusted limit (it fits on every save checked, but the adjusted time was logged only on WR26A, where no unit spoiled).

Facts checked in the game while building the probe, and the test runs behind them. Game facts about the save format, the clock and finances live in the [tf3-save-format notes](https://github.com/WuphonsReach/tf3-save-format/tree/00c4039160d26f4fd15df0fdaf9466e67c737f7d/docs); links to them here are pinned to one commit (see [README](README.md)).

## How pay works

- **Spoiled cargo pays full price in the base game.** Fresh, spoiled and mixed carts are all paid the same: the income function's result times a scale the engine applies afterwards. The scale is the Cargo Income setting times the company's ticket price multiplier (the Inflation option; it steps down with the earned rank and does not exist at rank 1). At rank 3 with Inflation Normal it is 0.96426 (`1 - 2 x 0.25 / 14`). The mod reads both at run time and falls back to 0.96426 if it cannot.
- **Pay is straight-line distance**: price factor x 3D distance between pickup and delivery (plus a climb term). The unloading point matters, so a cart that stops slightly further away earns slightly more.
- **The income function cannot see freshness.** `baseConfig.economy.cargoIncomeScript` can be replaced by a mod (only one mod at a time can), but it is called once per cargo unit with only `difficultyFactor`, the price factor and the distance. It is called before the unit's arrival event.
- **A delivered unit is paid once, at the consumer, and the pay is shared back along its chain** by a formula we do not know (the player's observation from play; the arrival event is a final delivery and `pickupPoints` lists the legs). The tf3-save-format notes saw the same thing in one delivery: one booking per carrier at one time, 82% to water ([finances.md](https://github.com/WuphonsReach/tf3-save-format/blob/00c4039160d26f4fd15df0fdaf9466e67c737f7d/docs/finances.md), "One delivery paid both lines"). On the large save, ledgers of first-leg vehicles received between 0.05 and 11 times the pay predicted for their own units, which fits a split along the chain.
- **The prediction of a unit's pay matches the player's ledger.** Derived pay over predicted pay at the player is 1.001 on both flat saves (earned rank 1 and 2), with the booked adjustments taken out. Per vehicle and per line it is scattered (0.92 to 1.21 on the small save; median 0.41 and 0.61 on the large one) because those ledgers get only their share of a chain's pay. On the large save, ledgers dominated by one cargo type showed steady ratios of 0.8 to 0.86 (air, tram, one rail and one water cargo) and 0.59 (another rail cargo); the cause is open, see [revisit-later](future/revisit-later.md).
- **The player's income exceeds the prediction on some saves.** A control run on the small save with no bookings ran 1.145 over its prediction and QZZZ 1.575, while the two flat saves match. Passenger income is not logged, so this may be income the arrivals do not cover; open.

## Cargo units and freshness

- **Each cargo unit is an entity** (`SimCargo`) with `startTime` (produced), `pickupTime`, a source entity, `deliveryExtensionDuration` and `pickupPoints`: one entry per vehicle and line the unit travelled on, each with its own carrier. `api.engine.util.cargo.isGoodQuality(unit)` tells whether it is spoiled.
- **The delivery limit is the game's adjusted delivery time, and it shrinks as the game years pass** (`api.engine.util.stock.getAdjustedDeliveryTime(cargoType)`, in seconds, the same for every cargo as a multiple of its file's `timeToDeliverInSeconds`). On WR26A it was 1.4 x through 1916 and 1.3936 x from Jan 1917 on (oil 4,200 s, then 4,181 s; the change came at the year boundary, for all eight cargo types at once), and the vehicles' average quality equalled `1 - age / adjusted time` to within 0.0006 on 130 arrivals ([line-billing.md](future/line-billing.md#results)). Older runs logged no adjusted time, but their spoiled flags give the limit (age over file time, units that did not wait in a warehouse): 1900, 1.4985 good and 1.5006 spoiled (29 units, 1,687,500 ms for vegetables and fish), on both the Normal save and its copy reloaded at Very High delivery sensitivity, so that setting does not move it; 2000 (333), good up to 0.8333 and spoiled from 0.8547; 2191 (Emerald Shores), good up to 0.7507 and spoiled from 0.7505. 1.5 - 0.00625 per year since 1900 fits 1900 to 1917 exactly but gives 0.875 for 2000, so the rule is not that straight line throughout. The mod used 1.5 x until 1.2, so its freshness on late saves ran far too high (on Emerald Shores a unit it rated 50% fresh was spoiled). The vehicle window's percentage is the load's average. The delivery times per cargo are in [delivery-times.md](https://github.com/WuphonsReach/tf3-save-format/blob/00c4039160d26f4fd15df0fdaf9466e67c737f7d/docs/delivery-times.md), spoilage in [spoilage.md](https://github.com/WuphonsReach/tf3-save-format/blob/00c4039160d26f4fd15df0fdaf9466e67c737f7d/docs/spoilage.md).
- **The limits differ by cargo.** The large save showed spoil limits from 1,687,500 ms (fish) to 6,750,000 ms; the mod reads each cargo type's own delivery time.
- **All eleven cargo types seen use the same price function.** Each cargo type's `scriptRef` is `incomeFactors.constantFn` with the same parameters (initial price 4, final price 3.2, decay rate 0.01, decay mod 0.1); only the decay start differs (100 for one cargo, 50 for the others).
- **Stored cargo disagrees with the plain formula.** On the large save, 124 units of one rail cargo and 38 of a road cargo were flagged good although the formula put them at 1.7 to 7 times past the limit; in one run 372 of 2,078 units had negative freshness by the formula and the game flagged 298 of them good (cargo types 6, 20 and 29 averaged -0.22, -1.93 and -4.71), and 1,501 units had an extension above zero. The player reports that a specialized warehouse stops the spoil timer ([warehouses.md](https://github.com/WuphonsReach/tf3-save-format/blob/00c4039160d26f4fd15df0fdaf9466e67c737f7d/docs/warehouses.md)).
- **`deliveryExtensionDuration` (logged as `ext`) looks like the time a unit's spoil timer was paused.** On the two large-save runs with the field (4,911 units), taking `ext` off a unit's age leaves none of the 4,000-odd good units past the limit (the plain age left 704), and every spoiled unit sits above about 0.845 of the limit on the corrected age (good units reach 0.844, spoiled start at 0.847; a window of 0.8444 to 0.8466 for cargo 6, 20 and 31 together). A constant-factor rule "age minus k x ext" did not fit all cargo types, so the exact spoil rule for paused cargo is not pinned down; the game's good flag is the authority for spoiled. Cargo 3, 18 and 32 never have an extension.

## What a mod can and cannot do

- **The cargo type's price function cannot be redirected from a mod at run time.** The `scriptRef` is readable through `api.res.cargoTypeRep.get(id)` but changing it has no effect.
- **Game script rules:** `sendCommand` must not be called from `handleEvent` (assertion `!m_betweenChanges`), journal entries must have `time = -1`, `sendCommand` must be called without a result callback from `update` (a callback raises "Callbacks are currently disallowed"), and module-level variables do not survive between calls, so state goes in the game script `state`. State written by the handler is visible to `update`.
- **A mod's `params` in `mod.json` become in-game settings.** They show under Mods (the mod's gear) in New Game and in Load Game, not under Gameplay Settings. Sliders with 21 and 41 values step in 5% and a list works; `numbers` is not passed to scripts. The game stores the choices in the save header, shows the save's own values when Load Game opens the dialog, and delivers an edited value at load. A script reads them with `api.engine.config.getModParams()`, keyed by mod id (the base game under the empty key), as 1-based list positions; `preRunFn` gets the same in `allModParams`. Checked on the probe params, five saves.
- **Asking the engine about a vanished entity aborts the whole event.** A vehicle sold while its cargo is still on the way makes `getComponent` raise "Invalid entity" (8 times in 15 minutes on the large save), which loses every unit of that event. Guard lookups with `api.engine.entityExists`, and read the carrier from the pickup point (`PickupPoint.carrier`), not from the vehicle.
- **A journal booking reaches the account.** `api.cmd.makeJournalBookAssetCmd` with an INCOME entry sent from `update` changes the player's balance by exactly the booked amount (negative for a deduction, positive for a bonus). Because there is no callback, success is judged from the balance; the entry's carrier is the row it appears under in the Finances tab.
- **Vehicles and lines have their own ledgers.** The first argument of `makeJournalBookAssetCmd` is the entity whose account the entry goes to; a vehicle, a line and the player each carry an `ACCOUNT` component, and an entry booked to one changes only that one (checked on the small save at 4x, 104 units: every tracked ledger moved by pay minus running costs minus the bookings, within 3%). The mod books to the player only, because pay is shared along the chain by an unknown formula (see [discarded](future/discarded.md)).
- **A mod script can load a module of its own with `ug_require "<mod id>::/file.tl"`.** The shorthand `::/file.tl` does not resolve in `mod.script`: a failing load there aborts initialisation and the game goes back to the main menu. A settings file loaded this way works in both `preRunFn` and the game script.
- **A mod can register its own notification type.** A `<name>.res.lua` (`type = "notification"`, `guiType`, `label`) beside a `<name>.script.tl` (`useDataState` returning title, description, icon, `lvmIcon`, click handlers) is picked up at game start; the type path `best_before_1::/<name>.script` resolves, and `Notifications` `updatePersistent` (sent from `update`) raises or clears a warning per entity. It shows as a pop-up icon, in the notification log and, through `lvmIcon`, in the [L] line manager, with a click that can select the line. The F2 statistics lists show a notification only if its `guiType` is `"Problem"`. The notification log row fits two lines of text (about 190 characters). See [stale-line-warnings.md](stale-line-warnings.md) (checked on two saves; one line warned, cleared and warned again).
- **Two copies of a mod with the same id: the staging copy wins.** With a `staging_area` folder and a subscribed mod.io copy both present the game logs `Multiple (2) mods with same id found <id>, the one from .../staging_area/<id>/ has been selected` and runs the staging copy; the Mods list shows one card. A test of the published file needs the staging link removed (seen once, 18 Oct 2026).
- **The small number on an activated mod's card in the Mods list is its activation order** (Mods tab, sorted by Activation Order): a save with the Deluxe pack and Best Before shows 1 and 2, and one with eleven mods numbers the cards 1 to 8 and on. Older screenshots show a ninth-mod save where activating the mod made it number 10.
- **A mod is published only through the game's own uploader** (Mod Hub, My Mods), which reads `_metadata/modinfo.json` for the listing; the mod.io website refuses Transport Fever 3 uploads. Details in [publishing.md](publishing.md).
- **Time in the journal is the game clock**, not the calendar: the game books on clock-tick periods whatever the calendar speed. A running-cost batch comes every 60,000 clock units and a year is 1,461,000 ([running-costs.md](https://github.com/WuphonsReach/tf3-save-format/blob/00c4039160d26f4fd15df0fdaf9466e67c737f7d/docs/running-costs.md), [calendar.md](https://github.com/WuphonsReach/tf3-save-format/blob/00c4039160d26f4fd15df0fdaf9466e67c737f7d/docs/calendar.md)).

Runs were played at calendar speed 4x or 0. A save is often replayed from the same starting point to try another curve or check performance; those runs repeat the same deliveries, so `tools/extract_run.py` groups runs by starting point and marks the largest of each group as primary. The seven primary runs (six games; the large flat game has two starting points; 63,264 units, 999 million base pay) are pooled in `tools/replay_curve.py` for the defaults (`--use-defaults`, since they were played on other curves).

## Test results

- **Deductions land.** Small save at 4x (8 game-minutes, 104 units, 992,034 in total): the account on screen matched the logged balance apart from the widget lagging a few seconds.
- **Ships, rail, trams and air are adjusted too.** A run with the water limit shrunk to a quarter (a test setting) deducted 54 ship units (197,529). On the large save, units on road, rail, tram and water were deducted (1,680 units, 18.7 million) with no unit lost between the arrival lines and the bookings. In the 333 run 354 units arrived on a last air leg: 331 earned a bonus and 23 spoiled ones took the full penalty.
- **Chains of three legs work.** Large save, 4x, about 4 minutes, 2,078 arrivals: 577 units travelled one leg, 1,242 two and 259 three (water > rail > tram, water > rail > rail, rail > rail > road, road > rail > road); no "Invalid entity" error, 928 units deducted (11.3 million) on road, rail, tram and water, all 928 sent to the player, 535 of 881 entries (61%) visible as an exact step of the balance or income total. Six screenshots matched the logged balance within 0.003%, two of them exactly.
- **Positive bookings land too.** Small save at 4x (about 3 minutes, 102 units, road and water): 42 bonus entries (156,258 added) and 60 deductions (973,483) went to the player; 97 of 102 entries (95%) showed up as an exact step of the balance or income total (bonuses 40 of 42, deductions 57 of 60). The account on screen matched the logged balance exactly in 7 of 10 screenshots and was within 45,000 in the rest (widget lag).
- **The full curve over six calendar years matches a no-mod control.** Emerald save, 4x, 13 minutes: Feb 2191 to Apr 2197, which is 6.2 calendar years but only 1.56 years of game clock (the clock does not follow the calendar speed). Curve: `bonusPercent` 0.5, `bonusDownToFreshness` 0.5, `breakevenFreshness` 0.3, `penaltyPercent` -0.95, `stored` corrected. 14,876 units adjusted (113.9 million of bonus, 5.3 million deducted), all sent, landing 58%, no errors. The same save played without the mod gives a control: the account grew 13.6 million per calendar year without the mod and 30.2 million with it, a difference of 16.6 million per year against the replay's 17.6 million. By Sep 2192 in the Finances tab, the extra earnings were road +19.6 million, rail +4.1 million, water +0.4 million against the log's net adjustment of 18.7 + 1.2 (tram books under the road row), 4.1 and 0.4 million. The account widget lagged the journal by 0 to 0.5 million, apart from two screenshots that caught a running-cost tick (about -5 million) one update late.
- **The panels move in lumps.** The line's balance panel only moves when a whole cart finishes unloading, and the account drops in lumps when running costs are booked for every vehicle together. A screenshot is a rough check; the log is the reference.
- **A deduction shows as a floating red number over the target building.** WR26A Giga-T 1900 (10 screenshots at 4x, 21:32 to 21:35 local, against the log): when a spoiled unit's deduction is booked, a small red negative figure floats over the building that took the delivery. The readable ones match the log's deductions for that moment to the dollar: $-12286 and $-12313 over the Sherman buildings at 01:33:00Z (log: predicted pay 12285.6 and 12313.0, deducted 12286 and 12313), and $-12425 later. A doubled figure (about $-24,572 and $-24,626, read from small text) is two carts unloading at the same truck stop at the same time: their deductions add up in one number. No floating figure for the engine's own pay was seen in these frames. A second series at Wigton (WIG Crops 1, line 185051, 19 screenshots, Sep 18 to Oct 17 1902) shows the same for a line that is stale-ish but not spoiled: $-1,223 over a building at 21:41:19 (log: net -1223 at 01:41:16Z) and $-2,692 at 21:40:39 (log: net -2692 at 01:40:40Z). The engine's own pay shows too, as a blue positive figure over the station stop when a cart finishes unloading: $74,068, $74,030 and $73,820 over the Wigton stop, about four units' predicted pay (the line's units are $18.2k to $18.9k each); the match is not exact. A bonus floats too, as a pale figure with no sign (colour not judged at this size). On a ship line (191956, cargo 6, +25%) the figures over the harbour town's buildings match the log's bonuses to the dollar, in two deliveries of 18 and 20 units a minute apart: $2,184, $2,172, $2,162, $2,104 and $2,102 at 01:48:17Z to 01:48:20Z (log +2184, +2172, +2162, +2104, +2102), and $2,116, $2,108 and $2,163 at 01:50:10Z to 01:50:12Z. Over the ship a larger figure shows ($154,414 against 18 units' predicted pay of $154,921, within 0.3%): the engine's pay for the whole load. Small red figures ($-205 to $-2,283) float over buildings and vehicles; the player says red negatives are usually vehicle maintenance, so only a red figure that matches a logged deduction to the dollar counts as ours. A small figure at a bus stop ($834) matches no logged unit; the player takes it for a passenger fare paid as passengers leave the carriage, which is the engine's own pay and not ours. The figures were read from screenshots scaled to 2000 px wide, so small ones are uncertain.
- **Rank 1 and Inflation High need no multiplier.** Large flat save (dry climate, Hard, Inflation High, rank 1, Cargo Income 100%), curve +50% / -95%, 1,572 units: the company has no ticket price multiplier (the log shows `nil`), the mod uses 1.0, and derived pay over predicted pay at the player is 1.001. A second flat save (earned rank 2, `potentialLevel`, while the claimed `level` was still 1) used 0.9714 for the whole run: the multiplier follows the earned rank, which the company window shows and the claimed level lags, and the prediction matched (1.001, 2,289 units).
- **Stored cargo on the flat saves.** 121 to 132 units of cargo 33 went rail, warehouse, road. Their plain freshness was about -0.45 (first save) and -1.8 to -2.6 (second, a longer wait) but the game flagged them good; corrected they scored 0.61 to 0.66. Judged on the plain age they would have lost $5.4M to $5.7M of $66M to $75M base pay.
- **A full (-100%) deduction lands.** QZZZ at 4x with +30% / -100%, 7,921 units, 2,850 flagged spoiled: every spoiled unit was deducted 99.99% to 100.01% of its predicted pay and none beyond rounding; no unit that was not spoiled was deducted. Net -24.2% of base pay. The verifier passes (7,921 units sent, 73% of the entries landed as an exact step because large deductions share steps with other pay). The player's account fell from $54.7M to -$7.9M and the game kept running; see [revisit-later](future/revisit-later.md).
- **The curve at the current defaults over the seven starting points.** +20% bonus from 60% of the limit used, break-even at 80% used, -100% penalty: pooled net +7.7% of $999 million base pay, per run from -30.0% (QZZZ) to +19.1% (flat). The same runs net +16.5% at +30% bonus and +34.7% at +50% / -95%. Replays in `tools/replay_curve.py`; the trade-off is in [revisit-later](future/revisit-later.md).
- **The 333 save with the curve switched off (a control).** Settings 0% bonus, 0% penalty: 14,989 units logged, no entries sent, the Account widget matched the journal at every screenshot. Replayed through +50% / -95% the same units net +41.0%, close to the +38.6% of the run that used that curve.
- **A long run with the in-game settings and the tools reading the curve from the log.** 333 save (large, Vice President, 1,249 vehicles; the save held no mods and the mod was activated on Load Game), set to 135% / 55% / 25% / 5% / 15% in the dialog, Apr to Dec 2000 at `logLevel` `curve`: `preRunFn` logged `loadParams` 8/12/6/2/4, the probe logged the same curve in `curveSettings`, the two saves made during the run hold the five settings. 12,321 units, all sent (6,973 entries): +56,973,205 bonus and -8,082,659 deduction logged, +27.6% of base pay. `extract_run.py` read the curve from `loadParams`, and `replay_curve.py` on it, with no flags, gave +56,973,296 and -8,082,636 (within $91 and $23). `verify_run.py` passed units, errors and journal gaps; the exact-step landing rate was 24% on this busy save (about 1.4 entries per snapshot, many pay events in each step), against 2 hits for the same amounts shifted by one, so the check now compares against that control. The `company` and `payScale` lines were written once each.
