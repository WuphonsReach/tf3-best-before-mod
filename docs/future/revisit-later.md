# Revisit once more is known

## In short

- **Open questions.** How the game splits a transferred unit's pay along its chain (ticket billing does not need it, see [line-billing.md](line-billing.md)); why player income exceeds the prediction on some saves (passenger fares on one save); what `deliveryExtensionDuration` equals exactly and how a universal warehouse counts; and what a negative account does over a longer time.
- **Open choices.** The curve defaults (+20% bonus, break-even at 80% of the delivery limit used, -100% penalty) net +7.7% pooled over seven starting points in six games, on runs that judged freshness against 1.5 x the delivery time (see Freshness below); whether to aim at about zero or reward good logistics waits on more saves.
- **Settled.** Deductions are never capped at the account balance. The log levels cost no measurable CPU, even `full` on a busy save (see the CPU note below); keep them for log size and bug reports.

Each item says what would settle it.

## Pay and ledgers

- **How the pay of a delivered unit is split along its chain.** Parked: ticket billing does not need it (the engine shares the answered pay itself), and the `bookings` fallback books to the player only. Settle with a small controlled save: one flow on a single leg, then two legs (one long, one short), then an intermediate industry. The log already names every vehicle, line and carrier of a unit's chain and the balance of each, so the shares can be read per delivery. The ticket price event does not show the split (one item per unit, naming only the last leg; see [line-billing.md](line-billing.md)).
- **Which carrier a deduction is booked under** (the `bookings` fallback). The last leg's carrier. The game seems to book one entry per carrier ([one delivery in the save-format notes](https://github.com/WuphonsReach/tf3-save-format/blob/00c4039160d26f4fd15df0fdaf9466e67c737f7d/docs/finances.md) split 82% water), so a split of the deduction by carrier may match the Finances tab better.
- **Why a unit's predicted pay is off by a steady factor per cargo type** on the large save (0.8 to 0.86 for several, 0.59 for another). The price functions are ruled out: all eleven cargo types seen use the same `constantFn` parameters (see [findings](../findings.md)). The likely cause is the split along the chain, which is parked (see the first item); the log records each unit's chain if it is picked up again.
- **Why player income exceeds the prediction on some saves** (1.145 on the small save without the mod, 1.575 on QZZZ; the two flat saves match at 1.001). Passenger fares, most likely: on WR26A the engine's pay was 1.442 x the predicted cargo pay and matched cargo + passenger ticket prices x the pay scale to 0.1% ([line-billing.md](line-billing.md#results)). Not yet checked on QZZZ or the small save.

## Freshness

- **Stored cargo.** The `corrected` mode (the only one offered, see [stored-cargo-modes.md](stored-cargo-modes.md)) takes `deliveryExtensionDuration` off a unit's age, which removes every disagreement with the game's good flag on the logged runs (see [findings](../findings.md)). The 1900 to 2032 run shows the corrected age flipping the flag at the adjusted limit ([delivery-limit-tests.md](delivery-limit-tests.md)). Open: what `ext` equals exactly, a universal warehouse, and how much stored cargo other saves have.
- **Replays on the game's limit.** The rule is settled (the file's time x `1.5 - (year - 1900) / 160` until 2020, x 0.75 from 2020 on; the spoiled flag flips at the age less `ext` against that limit, [delivery-limit-tests.md](delivery-limit-tests.md)). What is left is the old runs: the replays and curve defaults were tuned on runs that used 1.5 x, so on the 2000 and 2191 saves (333, Emerald Shores), where the game's limit was 0.875 x and 0.75 x, the logged freshness was too high and the replays should be redone with the game's limit. Open: whether anything but the year moves the limit beyond the settings tried, and a universal warehouse's effect on the age.

## The curve and its defaults

- **Shape.** Five settings with straight lines between them: bonus at 100% freshness, where the bonus starts to fall, break-even, where the penalty is at its maximum, and the penalty at 0% and when spoiled. A smooth curve could be added later as an option without changing the five settings.
- **Defaults.** Current: +20% bonus down to 40% freshness (60% of the limit used), break-even at 20% freshness (80% used), -100% penalty at 0% fresh and below. Replayed over the seven starting points of six games (a save replayed from the same start counts once; 999 million base pay) it nets +7.7% pooled (+5.0% without the 333 save, which is 45% of the pay); per run from -30.0% (QZZZ, 36% of units spoiled) to +19.1% (the flat saves, almost nothing spoils). The same runs net +16.5% at +30% / -100% and +34.7% at +50% / -95%. No single default nets zero: the bonus that does is 0% to 8% on saves that rarely spoil, 19% on the large save and 34% to 71% on saves that spoil a lot; pooled it is 11%. Most cargo is either very fresh or spoiled, so the fade points matter little near the defaults: the bonus fade point anywhere from 20% to 60% freshness and break-even from 0% to 40% move the pooled net by at most 1.6 points (only a bonus window narrowed to 80% or 100% freshness cuts it, to +5.4% and +2.2%); the bonus size sets the baseline and the penalty size decides how hard a badly run save is hit. Open: whether to aim at roughly zero (about +11% bonus at -100%) or reward good logistics (the current ones). Settle with more games, ideally one with Cargo Income at 150% (Emerald and 333, both rank 13, are already in the pool).
- **Bonus.** Bonuses are verified with ticket billing (answers of 2 and the curve's up to 1.5 reached every line of a chain, [line-billing.md](line-billing.md)) and with the bookings ([findings](../findings.md)). The bonus creates money the base game never pays, so the default stays a deliberate choice, not a side effect; the settings allow up to +200%.
- **Full-pay deductions.** The penalty allows -100%. With ticket billing the answer is never below 0, so such a unit pays exactly nothing. With the bookings the deduction is capped at 100% of the predicted pay. All 2,850 spoiled units on the QZZZ save were deducted 99.99% to 100.01% of their predicted pay, none beyond rounding. The prediction is good to about 1%, so a unit can still end a hair negative in the ledger when the prediction is a little high.

## The account can go negative

- **Decided: no cap.** The mod never holds back a deduction because the account is low. Its purpose is to penalize delivering spoiled cargo: the player is paid nothing for a spoiled unit, and with the bookings a prediction a hair too high can leave a unit a small fine (rounding-sized). A deduction that depended on the balance would also make pay depend on when a unit arrives.
- **What happened.** On the QZZZ save with the +30% / -100% curve the account fell from $54.7M to -$7.9M in one run (low point -$8.4M): -$26.1M of net adjustments on top of $205.7M of running costs. The game showed no error and the run continued.
- **Still unknown.** What the game does with a negative account over a longer time (loans, bankruptcy, vehicle sales). Only worth a longer run or a read of the game's bankruptcy rules if it turns out to matter for players.

## Housekeeping

- **The CPU cost of the log levels** is not measurable: all four levels cost about the same, on a light save and on a busy one. Busy save: the 333 save (large, Vice President, 1,249 vehicles, about 1,500 delivered units per clock month, no other mods; same file loaded for every run, 4x, game dates May to Sep 2000, 4 clock months or about 345 s of play per level, `tools/cpu_sample.py`). Simulation-thread CPU per game month: `off` 177 s, `summary` 168, `curve` 178, `full` 171 (a spread of 6%, and `off` was not the cheapest); all sampled threads 580 to 657% of one core; the game ran at the same speed in every run (about 2.8 real seconds per game day). What differs is the log size: about 8.8 MB per 6 minutes of play at `curve` and 15.6 MB at `full`, under 0.1 MB at `off` and `summary`. Light save: `full` against `off` on a save with about 270 delivered units per game month (Mar 1937 to Jul 1940, 4x, about 6 minutes each) cost +2.6% and +1.7% in the simulation threads. Run-to-run noise looks like about 7%, and the Main Pool (drawing, not our code) varied more than the Sim Pool, so only a large gap would count. Not tried: `curve` and `full` over many game years (the logs grow without limit).
- **The landing check in the verifier** (bookings only) is a rate of exact steps (about 0.6 to 0.96) because deductions can share a step with pay. A controlled save with one flow would allow an exact check.
