# Delivery limit tests (plan)

## In short

- **The question.** What sets a cargo's delivery limit (`api.engine.util.stock.getAdjustedDeliveryTime(cargoType)`), which the mod's freshness runs against. Known: the game year moves it (1.5 x the cargo file's time in 1900, 1.4 x in 1916, 1.39375 x in 1917, about 0.84 x in 2000 and 0.75 x in 2191, the last two inferred from spoiled flags, see [findings](../findings.md)); delivery sensitivity at Very High did not move the 1900 value. Not known: the rule after 1919, whether any other difficulty option moves it, and whether the vehicle type changes the age (the limit itself has no vehicle input). The spoiled flag flips at the limit to within 2 s (1919 grain, see the results).
- **Why it matters.** Every freshness figure, the curve defaults and the replays rest on the limit. Runs that assumed 1.5 x read too fresh on late saves.
- **How we think it works.** The function takes only a cargo type, so a vehicle cannot change the limit; it can change the age (the warehouse timer pause) and so the unit's freshness. Difficulty options are stored values and only the delivery sensitivity looks related to deliveries, so the year is probably the one driver. Low confidence until the tests below run.
- **Cheap setup.** The game lets a player change difficulty options on every load, so one save can be reloaded under each option with nothing else changed; no new game is needed.
- **Needs first.** A `deliveryLimit` log line (below); the other tests read it.

## Needed log line

One `BEST_BEFORE deliveryLimit` line per cargo type at the first update after a load and once per game month: game date (year and month), cargo type id, file `timeToDeliverInSeconds`, adjusted seconds, factor. `loadParams` already goes out once per load; the new line adds the factor beside it. Written by `logDeliveryLimits` in `probe.script.tl` at `curve` and `full` (not `summary`): when any adjusted time or the calendar year differs from the last line, and at least once per game month, so a reload inside the same game month adds no line until the month turns. `verify_run.py` and `extract_run.py` dispatch on the line's first word and skip the new type. The engine returns whole seconds, so the factor carries a rounding error of up to 1 s over the file time.

## Tests

Each test is one save, reloaded where the option changes, run for a few game months at speed. Read the `deliveryLimit` lines; units need not spoil.

1. **Factor against the date.** Load a save, run at 4x across several game years (a year at the boundary shows the step), and read the factor each month. Repeat from saves of other eras (1900, 1950, 2000, 2100, 2191). Gives the rule between the sampled years. Result to record: a table of date against factor, and whether the step happens at the year boundary only.
2. **Each difficulty option, same save.** Load one save once per setting, change one option per load, run one in-game month and read the factor. The options to try: delivery sensitivity (all seven levels, the 1900 test only used two), then the other sensitivities, inflation, vehicle costs, industry productivity, subsidies and anything else the load screen offers ([settings.md](https://github.com/WuphonsReach/tf3-save-format/blob/00c4039160d26f4fd15df0fdaf9466e67c737f7d/docs/settings.md) lists them). Same game date every time (reload from the same save, do not play on). Result: which option, if any, changes the factor.
3. **Specialized against universal vehicles.** Setup chosen by the player: two trains on the existing grain line, one with 50 km/h specialized cars and one with regular cars. One cargo, one route, two lines: one with a specialized vehicle (a hopper or tank wagon, a tanker) and one with a universal vehicle (a flat wagon or container, an open truck), same pickup and drop. Log at `curve` and compare per unit: the adjusted limit (expected equal), the age at arrival, `good`, and the vehicle's cargo bar. Also check whether cargo spoils differently in a universal vehicle at a long route. Result: limit equal or not; whether the spoiled flag follows the same boundary.
4. **Spoil boundary.** On a late save (2000 or later, factor well under 1.4) where units do spoil, log `good` against `1 - age / adjusted` and `corrected`. Result: the largest freshness with `good = false` and the smallest with `good = true`, per cargo type. Settles whether the flag flips at the adjusted limit.
5. **Warehouse timer (specialized against universal warehouse).** Same cargo through a specialized warehouse and a universal one, same wait. Compare `deliveryExtensionDuration` to the wait. Settles the player-reported pause ([spoilage.md](https://github.com/WuphonsReach/tf3-save-format/blob/00c4039160d26f4fd15df0fdaf9466e67c737f7d/docs/spoilage.md)).

## Test results

### Grain line, specialized train (WR26A, Feb to Apr 1919, Hard difficulty, `curve`; log `local/logs/stdout-20261025-delivery-limit-grain.txt`)

- **The factor in 1919 is 1.38125, cut to whole seconds.** All 31 cargo types logged `adjustedSeconds` equal to `floor(file time x 1.38125)` (grain 1,286 s gives 1,776; 900 gives 1,243; 1,125 gives 1,553; 4,500 gives 6,215). 1.5 - 0.00625 x (year - 1900) now fits 1900, 1916, 1917 and 1919; only the 2000 reading (about 0.84, from spoiled flags) is off it. One calendar year in the run, so no year step was seen, and the logged `factor` column is the truncated value over the file time (1.38044 to 1.38111).
- **Specialized train (Gondola Car, Bulk, 10 t a car, 50 km/h; Prussian T 3; 64 units).** Grain from Cranbrook to Dereham, a line whose limit is 29.6 min and whose trip took 31.3 to 32.1 min. All 64 units arrived spoiled (`good=false`, age 1,879,200 to 1,928,200 ms against a 1,776,000 ms limit, freshness -0.058 to -0.086, `rate=0`, 47,513 deducted each, 3.04 million in all). The cargo bar in the vehicle window ran 97% at departure, 91%, 82%, 63%, 45%, 33%, 18%, 7% and 1%; the spoiled counter showed 8 units at 1%, 62 at 0% and all 64 on arrival (the units loaded first pass the limit first, which matches a flip at the limit but does not pin it: no unit arrived near 1.0).
- **Universal train (Cargo Car 1899, All Cargo, 10 t, 50 km/h; 64 units; log `local/logs/stdout-20261025-delivery-limit-grain2.txt`).** No delays on the way: ages 1,724,600 to 1,795,600 ms against the same 1,776,000 ms limit, so 53 units arrived good and 11 spoiled. The specialized train's trip (1,879,200 to 1,928,200 ms) was about 150 s longer, so the cars are not what made the difference; the first train waited longer. The limit is the same for both car types (one `adjustedSeconds` per cargo type, no vehicle input).
- **The spoiled flag flips at the limit (within 2 s).** In the 128 grain units of both trains no spoiled unit has a corrected freshness above -0.0014 (age 1,778,600 ms) and no good unit below -0.0003 (age 1,776,600 ms); the unrounded limit is 1,776.29 s (1,286 x 1.38125) and the log's ages come in 200 ms steps. Over the whole run (7,129 arrivals, 75 spoiled) no spoiled unit has freshness at or above 0, and only two good units sit below it (the largest -0.0003). Rounding the limit down to whole seconds has no visible effect.
- **Screenshots.** The universal train's cargo bar fell from about 44% (1919-04-10 07:12) to 4% at the unload and the spoiled-count face came up while it unloaded; the pictures are too small to read the counts, so the log carries the numbers.
- **Next.** Test 4 (the boundary) is answered for this save and year. Test 3 (specialized against universal) needs two trains that start together and take the same time, since the trip time, not the car type, is what differed here. The factor against the date needs saves of other years (the 2000 and 2191 readings are inferred).

### Same save on Easy (1919-04-10, difficulty Easy chosen on the Load Game screen with debug mode on; log `local/logs/stdout-20261025-delivery-limit-easy.txt`)

- **The limit does not move with the difficulty level.** The first `deliveryLimit` lines after the load read grain 1,776 s, the 900 s class 1,243 s and the 4,500 s class 6,215 s, the same as on Hard. The preset changes delivery sensitivity (now Low) with the other options; the engine's adjusted delivery time ignores all of them. Delivery sensitivity Off and Extreme, and the other options one at a time, are not tried.
- **Specialized train, 64 units:** all good, ages 1,711,800 to 1,770,200 ms against the unchanged 1,776,000 ms limit, freshness +0.0033 to +0.0361, ticket answers 0.0218 to 0.241 of the pay (the curve's low end), load and unload windows 31.6 s each, as on Hard. The trip was about 160 s shorter than the Hard run's first train (1,879,200 to 1,928,200 ms), which had stood waiting for a free path; the universal train's Hard trip (1,724,600 to 1,795,600 ms) is close to this one. No unit reached the limit, so this train says nothing new about the boundary.
- **Payout against the screenshot.** The specialized train's income bar for 10/4/1919 reads $522,770 (tooltip in the vehicle window, screenshot 07:37:34 local). The log's estimate for the 64 units is $522,329 (full pay $3,953,079 less the $3,430,750 the curve took), 0.08% under. At freshness +0.003 to +0.036 the curve paid about 13% of full pay.
- **Universal train on Easy (64 units; log `local/logs/stdout-20261025-delivery-limit-easy2.txt`):** 58 good, 6 spoiled; ages 1,688,400 to 1,801,400 ms; load and unload windows 63.0 s each, twice the specialized train's, as on Hard. The flag flips at the same place as on Hard: the oldest good unit is 1,776,400 ms (freshness -0.0002), the youngest spoiled 1,778,400 ms (-0.0013); over all 5,219 arrivals of the run, 6 spoiled and none at freshness 0 or above, the lowest good -0.0002. Its income bar reads $503,703 (screenshot 07:46:12 local) against the log's $503,270, 0.09% under. The pay the curve left is about 13% of full pay again, since the freshness sits at zero (the 6 spoiled units paid nothing).
- **Reading across Hard and Easy.** Same limit (1,776 s), same flip point (within 2 s of 1,776.29 s), same 2:1 load and unload windows, and the payout follows the ticket answer both times. Difficulty level changed none of them.
